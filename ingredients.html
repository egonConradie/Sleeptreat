The little things inside

Each ingredient has a job

Each ingredient plays a practical part in flavour, texture or formulation. These descriptions draw on food-science literature and will be checked against the final recipe and supplier documentation.

Carob powder
What it is

A finely milled powder made from carob pods.

What it does

Adds deep colour and a naturally sweet, roasted flavour to the treat.

Soy crisps
What it is

A light, extruded soy-based crisp inclusion.

What it does

Creates the airy crunch and crisp contrast in every bite. Approved soy allergen wording is still required.

Magnesium bisglycinate
What it is

A mineral ingredient in which magnesium is bound to two glycine molecules.

What it does

Provides the formulation’s added magnesium component; elemental magnesium per treat is still being verified.

Oat flour
What it is

Finely milled oats.

What it does

Absorbs moisture and adds body, helping create a consistent, cohesive texture.

Brown rice flour
What it is

Flour milled from whole-grain brown rice.

What it does

Adds fine solids and starch that help build body and structure in the base.

Chicory root
What it is

A chicory-root ingredient; its exact supplied form is being confirmed.

What it does

Chicory ingredients can add bulk and bind water, helping round out body and mouthfeel.

Pumpkin seed butter
What it is

A smooth paste made from milled pumpkin seeds.

What it does

Brings an earthy, nutty flavour and a creamy base that helps bring the dry ingredients together.

Literature-informed · formulation confirmation pending

These are common food-science functions, not measured claims about the finished Sleep Treat. Final wording should be matched to supplier specifications, the confirmed recipe, analytical results and applicable South African labelling requirements.

Curious minds

Ingredient questions

Why magnesium bisglycinate?
It is the formulation’s selected source of added magnesium. The supplier specification and elemental magnesium per serving are being confirmed before any nutrition or sleep-related claim is published.
Why use carob?
Carob brings the recipe its deep colour and gently sweet, roasted flavour. The final sensory description will be checked against the finished formulation.
What gives Sleep Treat its crunch?
The soy crisps create the light, crisp bite, while the flour-and-seed-butter base helps hold the pieces together.
Why use chicory root?
Chicory-derived ingredients can add bulk and influence body and mouthfeel. The exact supplied form and its role in this recipe are still being matched to the specification.
What makes the formulation different?
The recipe combines crisp soy inclusions, a seed-butter base, a carob-led flavour system and an added magnesium ingredient in one individually wrapped treat. Comparative claims will wait for the final project analysis.
